# Data processing agreement

**Version 2026-11-04.5.** This is an English translation of the Spanish contract, provided for convenience; in case of discrepancy, the Spanish version prevails. It forms part of the CheckBiz360 Terms of Service and governs the processing of personal data that ENCODEBIZ SL carries out on behalf of each client company, in accordance with Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR) and Spanish Organic Law 3/2018 (LOPDGDD).

## 1. Parties and contact

**Data controller:** the company identified when signing up for the service with its company name, tax ID and address, represented by the person who accepts this agreement on its behalf ("the Company" or "the Controller").

**Data processor:** ENCODEBIZ SL, tax ID B24873275, with registered office at Calle Alondra, 3, 28025 Madrid ("EncodeBiz" or "the Processor"), owner of CheckBiz360. Contact for instructions, data subject rights and incidents: soporte@encodebiz.com.

This agreement is accepted by electronic means in the CheckBiz360 dashboard (Art. 28.9 GDPR). For each acceptance, EncodeBiz keeps a record of who accepted it, on behalf of which company, when, the version accepted and from which connection. In matters of data protection, it prevails over any incompatible condition of the main contract or of the commercial documentation.

## 2. Subject matter and duration

The engagement covers the hosting, maintenance and support of CheckBiz360 and the processing needed for the functions that the Company contracts and expressly activates. It lasts as long as the service does and, afterwards, only for the return or deletion operations of clause 10 and for any retention required by law. The duty of secrecy has no end date.

## 3. Operations and purposes

The operations are collection, recording, organisation, consultation, storage, modification, extraction, disclosure to the authorised sub-processors, backup, restriction and erasure. Their purposes are: managing users and teams; recording working time; organising shifts, holidays and absences; delivering employment documents and keeping evidence of their receipt or signature; assigning tasks and checking their execution; managing materials and inventory; managing the Company's customer portfolio; and, when the Company activates them, providing operational indicators and assistance through artificial intelligence.

The use of workforce data for advertising, marketing, model training or purposes of EncodeBiz's own or of its sub-processors is not authorised. To operate and improve the service, EncodeBiz only uses truly anonymous metrics, which do not allow any person or company to be identified or singled out. Pseudonymisation on its own does not make data anonymous. If EncodeBiz used the data for its own purposes, it would become the controller of that processing (Art. 28.10 GDPR and Art. 33.2 LOPDGDD).

## 4. Persons and data covered

**Data subjects:** the Company's workers, including interns or trainees it registers; owners, administrators, managers and other authorised users of the dashboard; and, if they are legitimately included in tasks, documents or the customer portfolio, the Company's customers and third parties related to its activity.

**Data:** those of **Annex I**. In summary: identification and contact details, position, work site, role and status; clock-ins, schedules, shifts, leave and absences; one-off position when clocking in, distance to the work site and device data; photographs from the clock-in point and the tablet's position; tasks, notes, photos, videos and ratings; operational indicators and profiles; assigned equipment; payslips, contracts, communications and private documents; signatures and their records; and whatever the Company queries through the artificial intelligence assistant.

The "sick leave" status, absences of the "medical" type and the documents that the Company uploads may contain health data or other special categories. They are limited to what is necessary for legitimate employment purposes: the service neither asks for nor records diagnoses or clinical information, and the absence type is only used to plan and to block clocking in on that day. Including data on criminal convictions or offences is not authorised.

## 6. Geolocation, photographs and identification

6.1. **Position when clocking in.** The device's position is obtained at the moment of clocking in, including rejected attempts, and when starting, resuming or completing a task that has its own location. There is no continuous tracking or capture of the position outside those actions, and the service does not allow it. The position of each clock-in is kept together with the working-time record it belongs to, for the same period as that record (clause 10), unless the Company instructs a different period in writing. It is for the Company to justify the necessity and proportionality of geolocation as a monitoring measure (Art. 20.3 of the Workers' Statute) and to inform the workforce in advance in accordance with Article 90 LOPDGDD.

6.2. **Photograph at the clock-in point.** If the Company activates the clock-in point on a shared device at the work site, a photograph is taken at each clock-in so that an authorised person of the Company can check, when needed, that the right person is clocking in. No system analyses the photograph: there is no facial recognition, it is not compared with anything and it is not sent to any analysis provider; the only automatic check happens on the device itself before the shot is taken, to verify that there is a face in the frame, and it neither stores nor sends anything. Photographs are kept for **30 days** by default or for the period the Company configures among those available; a period longer than usual requires the Company to justify it. Disputed photographs are set aside from automatic deletion until the dispute is resolved. Activating this function requires the Company to provide specific justification and to assess less intrusive alternatives.

6.3. **Biometric identification.** If the Company requires identity verification when clocking in, the fingerprint or face is checked by the worker's own mobile phone system: CheckBiz360 only receives the result of the check and never receives or stores biometric templates. A change to biometric identification on the platform would require prior review and a specific agreement; it is not authorised by this agreement.

## 7. Indicators and artificial intelligence assistants

7.1. The indicators of the operational analysis (compliance with working-time recording, deviation of hours from what was planned, clock-ins away from the work site, reliability and efficiency) and the groupings of people that result from them constitute profiling when they evaluate personal aspects. The Company should only use them after documenting their purpose and necessity, informing the workforce and assessing the impact assessment of Article 35 GDPR; the combination of profiling, workplace monitoring, location and health data requires particular assessment. EncodeBiz provides the Company with the logic, the variables, the limitations and the expected consequences of those indicators so that it can fulfil its information obligations, including Article 64.4.d of the Workers' Statute.

7.2. Taking decisions based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effects on workers is not authorised. Evaluations require effective human review and the possibility of correcting errors. Health data is not used to penalise or to infer reliability or performance, and the indicators do not use it.

7.3. The operational analysis is produced with a language model provider contracted by EncodeBiz, which acts as sub-processor (Annex II). Before sending, people's names are replaced by codes and restored when the response is received; the mapping between code and person does not leave EncodeBiz. Pseudonymised data remains personal data and is treated as such. The provider does not use it to train its models and does not keep it beyond the abuse-monitoring periods of its policy. The Company can deactivate the operational analysis from the dashboard.

7.4. The artificial intelligence assistant (Claude or ChatGPT) that the Company may connect is contracted by the Company with its own account and its own terms with that provider; EncodeBiz does not activate it, does not instruct it and only gives it access with the session of the Company's user. Connecting it is a decision of the Company, which must first check that provider's terms, role and transfers with respect to the data it queries through it. That provider is not among EncodeBiz's sub-processors.

## 8. Security, assistance and personal data breaches

8.1. EncodeBiz applies technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk in accordance with Article 32 GDPR, described in **Annex III**, and reviews them periodically. Health data, employment documents, photographs and positions are protected with restricted access and minimisation.

8.2. **Data subject rights.** The Company itself handles, through the dashboard, its workers' rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability: it can consult, correct, export and delete their data, subject to the limits imposed by the mandatory retention of working-time records. If a data subject exercises a right before EncodeBiz, EncodeBiz forwards it to the Company, to the account email, immediately and never later than the next working day, with the information received, and does not reply on its own unless instructed by the Company. EncodeBiz facilitates searching, access, rectification, export, restriction and erasure in all systems where the data is held, so that the Company can meet the legal deadlines.

8.3. **Personal data breaches.** EncodeBiz will notify the Company, at the account email, of any security breach affecting data processed on its behalf, whatever its severity, without undue delay and **within 24 hours at most** of becoming aware of it, even if the investigation has not concluded. It will report the nature of the breach, the data and the estimated number of persons affected, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed and a contact point; it will complete the information progressively, preserve the evidence and cooperate in the notifications and communications of Articles 33 and 34 GDPR. Assessing whether the breach entails a risk to individuals, and therefore whether it must be notified to the Spanish Data Protection Agency or communicated to the data subjects, is the Company's responsibility; if the Company so requests in writing, EncodeBiz will itself make the communication to the workers through the service.

8.4. **Impact assessments.** EncodeBiz assists the Company, with the information at its disposal, in the risk analysis, the impact assessment, the prior consultation with the supervisory authority and compliance with Articles 32 to 36 GDPR. Annex I describes the processing in the detail needed for that assessment.

8.5. **Demonstrating compliance and audits.** EncodeBiz makes available to the Company the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with this agreement, including the record of activities of clause 5.4 and the description of the security measures. The Company, itself or through an independent auditor who is not a competitor of EncodeBiz and is bound by confidentiality, may audit compliance with this agreement at reasonable intervals (no more than once a year, unless there are indications of non-compliance or a request from an authority), with 30 days' notice, or 5 working days' notice if there is a security breach or well-founded indications of non-compliance, during working hours and without interrupting the service to other clients. EncodeBiz may provide recent reports or certifications to facilitate the audit; if the Company considers them insufficient for what it needs to verify, the audit is carried out. The coordination and confidentiality of audits do not prevent the necessary checks or urgent actions or those of the authorities. Both parties provide the supervisory authority with the information in this clause that it requires of them.

## 10. Retention, return and deletion

10.1. The Company sets the retention periods by category of data. Working-time records are kept for **four years** in accordance with Article 34.9 of the Workers' Statute; the position of each clock-in is kept with its record unless the Company instructs otherwise (clause 6.1); clock-in point photographs, for the period of clause 6.2; documents, tasks and the other data, for as long as the service lasts and the Company does not delete them.

10.2. EncodeBiz hosts on behalf of the Company the electronically signed documents, with their timestamp and signature record, and allows them to be exported together. The Company keeps that documentation for the employment or legal-defence periods applicable to each document and instructs its retention, return or deletion when the service ends.

10.3. **When the service ends**, the Company chooses between the return of its data in a usable format and its subsequent deletion, or direct deletion. EncodeBiz asks for that choice at the end and carries out the operation without delay, within a **maximum of 30 calendar days** from termination, unless a shorter legal period or documented agreement applies. The Company's failure to reply does not allow indefinite retention: once that period has elapsed, EncodeBiz deletes. During the service and until that deletion, the Company can export its data from the dashboard in open formats (Excel, CSV, PDF). Deletion covers the associated data, the copies in all of EncodeBiz's systems, the files and the integrations under its control, including those of the sub-processors, and EncodeBiz issues a certificate of compliance at the Company's request.

10.4. Backups containing the Company's data are isolated and kept out of ordinary use until they are removed by rotation, within a maximum of 90 days from deletion; if one had to be restored, the Company's data would be deleted again. EncodeBiz only keeps, duly blocked and at the disposal of judges, courts, the Public Prosecutor's Office and public administrations, the data necessary while liabilities may arise from the provision of the service (Art. 33.4 LOPDGDD), and whatever a law obliges it to keep, for a defined period.

10.5. Removing a worker from the platform revokes their access; it does not mean the immediate erasure of the records that the Company must keep by law, which follow the rules of this clause.

## Annex III. Technical and organisational measures

General description of the measures, in accordance with Articles 28.3.c and 32 of the GDPR. The details of their implementation are confidential security information of EncodeBiz; they are made available to the Company, under confidentiality, within the framework of an audit under clause 8.5 or at the request of the supervisory authority.

- **Isolation per company.** Each client company's data is separated from that of the others in all systems, and every access is checked against the company it belongs to.
- **Access control.** Role-based access defined by the Company (owner, administrator, manager, viewer; employee in the application) and mandatory second authentication factor for the dashboard, the assistant and the workers; sensitive operations require express authorisation.
- **Identification.** Biometric verification, when the Company requires it, takes place on the worker's device; the platform neither receives nor stores biometric templates. Clock-in point photographs are seen only by authorised users of the Company and expire.
- **Confidentiality and minimisation.** Pseudonymisation of the data sent to language model providers; the position is recorded only once per clock-in; only the data necessary for the functions the Company activates; notifications and technical logs without sensitive content.
- **Encryption.** In transit in all communications; at rest in the cloud hosting; additional encryption of EncodeBiz's own for integration secrets and clock-in point codes.
- **Infrastructure security.** Restricted and authenticated administrative access, closed network except for the published services, complete separation between production and test environments, secrets managed outside the code.
- **Traceability.** Change history of clock-ins, clock-in point log, logging of support staff access to client data, of email sending, of electronic signatures and of acceptances of the legal texts.
- **Staff.** Confidentiality commitment, access limited to what is necessary and data protection training.
- **Continuity and recovery.** Daily backup of the main database, kept for 30 days, with point-in-time recovery; recovery procedures for the other systems.
- **Incident management.** Procedure for detection, containment, notification to the Company within the period of clause 8.3 and documentation of each security breach.
- **Review.** Periodic assessment of the effectiveness of the measures, of the access granted and of the revocation of departed users.
